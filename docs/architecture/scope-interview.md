# GarSync v2 — scope interview (decision register)

> One question at a time, answered by the owner in session on 2026-09-24. Each answer is registered
> here with what it changes, then folded into the design and the ADRs in pass 3. Nothing here is
> implemented until then.

| ID | Decision | Answer | What it changes | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SC-01** | Nutrition in the MVP | **Deferred.** v2 MVP = cross-referencing weight + body composition (FitDays) with Garmin training/recovery. The adaptive-intake loop is a later phase. | Drops phase 5 from the MVP. The `nutrition_entries` table and the weekly adjustment leave the critical path; the ODbL question (ADR-007 §6) becomes a *later* concern, not a v2 blocker. What ships is the **cross-reference**: body composition against training load and recovery, which is exactly the join no vendor does for you. | answered |
| **SC-02** | Expected data latency | **Accepted as normal, not as failure.** FitDays may upload hours late; Garmin revises sleep/HRV/training-readiness during the morning. | Three consequences the design must carry: (1) every ingest run **re-derives a trailing window** (start at 14 days) rather than only "today", because revisions and late arrivals must overwrite what was computed from incomplete data; (2) a measurement lands on its **own timestamp's local day**, never on its arrival day; (3) the UI and the digest must distinguish *"not uploaded yet"* from *"no data"*, with an expected-by time per metric class, and the staleness alert must be tuned above the normal lateness so a routine late upload never pages. | answered |
| **SC-03** | MVP boundary and schema preparation | **(a) + a minimal `goals` row.** Nutrition is out and **nothing is prepared for it**: no `nutrition_entries`, no kcal/protein `targets` — a later Alembic migration is routine and an unwritten table invites a half-built feature. The one exception is a `goals` table (goal weight, rate band, effective-from), because the `Body` screen's trend needs an axis to be drawn against, and that is not a nutrition concern. Empty placeholder tables were explicitly rejected. | ADR-009's data model; the definition of done for the scale phase; the `Body` screen's work. It also removes the ODbL/public-use question from the v2 critical path entirely (ADR-007 §6 keeps it for later). | answered |
| **SC-04** | Application surface | **Two routes, one deployment, no public data surface.** Route A (day to day): a name that resolves inside the VPN to the VPS tailnet IP, carrying `secure-headers` + `rate-limit` + `crowdsec-bouncer` + `vpn-whitelist` + `authelia` at `one_factor` with a long session — no prompt in practice, nothing reachable from the internet. Route B (devices that cannot join the mesh): the same app on a public name behind `authelia` at `two_factor`. Nothing is visible unauthenticated; only the login page is public. | ADR-010 and ADR-011: the access model becomes "two routes, one deployment" rather than "public or private". `trusted_cidrs` already contains `100.64.0.0/10` (`infra/config/values/common.yaml:53-57`), so the `one_factor`-for-tailnet policy is a config line, not new machinery. **Two verification items**: (i) confirm that a phone on 4G reaches the app *through the tunnel* (source `100.64.x.x`) — proven for LAN/VPN source addresses, not yet for a tailnet name in prod; (ii) `vpn-whitelist` fails closed. `make seed` stays a phase-0 deliverable as **DX** (CI smoke and screenshots need it because `make smoke` currently demands live Garmin credentials) — it is not a public demo. | answered |
| **SC-05** | **No overnight wear (constraint, discovered in Q3)** | The owner "rarely sleeps with the watch". Consequence: sleep stages/score, overnight HRV (`lastNightAvg`, weekly average, baseline), resting HR, overnight Body Battery recharge and therefore Garmin Training Readiness are **absent on most days**. The recovery pillar becomes *conditional on data existing* rather than a phase-1 deliverable. | The design's recovery half rested on an unverified behavioural assumption — recorded here as a finding against the design, not against the owner. Reorders the metric layer (load + body composition first, recovery conditional), keeps the `daily_metrics` columns but born NULL, and makes "did not sleep with the watch" an **expected** state in the coverage indicator rather than a missing-data error. Also removes the sleep-debt and sleep-regularity metrics from the MVP. What survives without night wear: all-day stress, steps, and every activity-derived metric (TRHIMP/hrTSS, CTL/ATL/TSB, ramp rate, Training Effect, VO2max, race predictions). | answered |
| **SC-06** | Recovery handling and the morning hero (was Q3) | **(a) accepted, with (c) as an optional later add-on.** The recovery pillar is *"when there is data"*, never the protagonist. The hero becomes the **relationship**: how weight, body fat and lean mass respond to accumulated training load (28-day weight/body-fat trend against accumulated load; lean-mass retention while volume rises). Recovery metrics contribute the days they exist. The optional add-on is a **Health Snapshot** morning ritual (on-demand HR/HRV/stress/respiration in ~2 minutes) instead of sleeping with the watch. | The `Today` screen and the digest hero change from "how you arrive today" to "what your body is doing with the training you give it". Reinforces why the MVP is a **cross-reference** product. `goals` (SC-03) becomes load-bearing rather than cosmetic: the band is what makes the relationship readable. **Verification item**: does the owner's watch model support Health Snapshot, and does the pinned `garminconnect` expose it? If either answer is no, (c) is dropped and nothing else changes. | answered |
| **SC-07** | Delivery cadence (was Q4) | **Weekly review on Sunday + a staleness alert, and nothing else.** The weekly review carries the load↔body-response relationship, the week's sessions and **one** adjustment sentence for the next week. A daily brief was considered and rejected for now: with the hero moving on a weekly timescale and no training plan in the system, a daily message would be short or empty most days and would train the owner to ignore the channel — including the one alert that matters. | The digest template (design §11.1) becomes a weekly shape, not a daily one. **PWA and push drop below the metric layer in priority** — they stop being a phase of their own and become a wrapper on the dashboard, which pulls the metric layer earlier in the plan. The staleness alert remains the only push, and SC-02's latency tolerance is what sets its threshold (settled in SC-10). | answered |
| **SC-08** | Backfill depth (was Q5) | **"Everything that can be had from Garmin and FitDays, the maximum that is useful for history"** — translated below into rules, because "everything" has three different costs and only one of them is worth paying. | Four rules: **(1) Summaries are cheap and complete.** Full Garmin activity history and daily wellness backfilled as far as the API paginates; FitDays cloud history in full (~400 days). This is the data the hero actually needs, it is small, and it is what makes the app useful on day one instead of in three months. **(2) Streams are fetched lazily, not backfilled wholesale.** Detail samples are re-queryable from Garmin for old activities, so the design inverts: fetch eagerly for a recent window (so efficiency/decoupling trends have no gaps once those metrics land) and on demand for any older activity the owner opens, then persist. The 90-day retention window becomes a *cache* policy rather than a data-loss boundary, and "maximum history" stops costing gigabytes. **(3) Raw-payload retention is asymmetric, and this falls out of the source's fragility**: Garmin is re-queryable, so its raw payloads can be short-lived; the FitDays cloud is a reverse-engineered surface that may disappear, so **its raw payloads are kept permanently** (one small row per weigh-in). **(4) Backfill is a one-off, throttled, resumable command that never blocks deployment** — the app serves partial history while it runs, and the ledger cursor of ADR-008 is what makes resumption safe. | Makes the date-filtered, paginated fetch **a phase-0 requirement** rather than an improvement, along with rate-limit throttling. Reinforces ADR-012's split: the FitDays cloud exists precisely to supply history, while BLE can only start from today. Reshapes the retention question in ADR-008 §9 and the disk ceiling in ADR-011 §9. | answered |
| **SC-09** | Scale route (was Q6) | **Cloud-first, and BLE drops out of the MVP** — *conditional on the no-phone test*. The owner reports that the scale already syncs to the FitDays app and uploads, so the question was never "BLE or cloud" but "is the cloud primary or only the backfill". Since the cloud poller is required anyway as the sole source of the ~400 days of history (SC-08), making it primary removes a process, all hardware, the GPL process boundary and about a week of work. | If it holds, ADR-012 flips from "BLE primary + cloud backfill" to "cloud primary, BLE only as a remedy", and the 10-minute BLE identification scan is **no longer needed** — it only existed to choose a BLE route. `weight_measurements` still stores raw weight and impedance, and the FitDays raw payloads are kept permanently (SC-08 rule 3), so the door to a later local algorithm stays open. **Verification item (M4)**: weigh in with the phone in another room, then open the app — does the record exist **with the original weigh-in timestamp**? If it appears only on app open, or not at all, then weigh-ins without the phone are lost and the gaps become a product decision (a capture device, or accepted gaps surfaced by the system). Recorded as conditional precisely because this is the same class of unmeasured behavioural dependency that cost the recovery pillar in SC-05. | answered conditionally |
| **SC-10** | Alerting scope (was Q7) | **Alert on system health, never on human behaviour.** One push class: the ingest is broken — a failed run, or no successful run within a threshold set **above** the normal upload lateness that the no-phone test measures. No "you have not weighed in for N days" nudges, no per-metric alerts. Data absence is surfaced where it belongs — the coverage line of the weekly review (SC-07). | Design §10/§11 lose the "no weigh-in gap" alert and keep exactly one alarm; §11.1's coverage slot becomes the only place a human-visible gap appears. The distinction the owner accepted is precise: the alarm watches **whether the ingest runs succeed**, never whether new data exists — so a routine late upload cannot produce a false positive. | answered |
| **SC-11** | LLM coach (was Q8) | **No LLM in v2.** The weekly review is deterministic: a template, the numbers, and **one** adjustment sentence driven by a rule over load versus body response. Every phrase is traceable to its inputs. Local narration (Ollama) is the evolution to consider only if the deterministic review proves insufficient; a hosted API is rejected outright. | **Supersedes ADR-005 in intent**, explicitly, because that ADR is live and proposes the opposite (snapshotting data to a hosted LLM at `POST /api/ai/chat`). ADR-013 already carried the intent; SC-11 turns it into a decision. It also removes the phase-6 agent/MCP surface from v2 scope. Two reasons recorded so the door is not reopened by accident: an LLM adds **prose, not insight** to an arithmetic review; and Ollama lives on `ace2`, an on-demand homelab node (ADR-028), so a fixed Sunday job would inherit "the report did not run because the PC was off". If an LLM ever enters, it **narrates, never prescribes**. | answered |
| **SC-12** | Other people's data (was Q9) | **One person, forever.** No subject/tenant column in the schema. If another person ever wants this app, they get a **second instance** — same repository, separate deployment, separate database, their own Authelia user. The owner's `owner_id`-as-insurance idea was considered and **withdrawn by the author**: a column whose value is always `1` is dead weight no test can prove correct, and it invites exactly the cross-tenant leak that a second deployment cannot have. | ADR-007 and ADR-010 stand unchanged with their rejection triggers intact, and the schema of ADR-009 gets simpler (no subject column on any table, no policy layer). The trigger that would reopen this stays as ADR-010 states it — *a second person's own data must live in the system* — and even then the answer is a second instance, not tenancy. | answered |
| **SC-13** | **Data density (side-effect of a misread question, and worth keeping)** | **The owner trains daily and weighs daily.** | This is the best case for the design and it retires several assumptions at once: (1) daily weigh-ins make the EMA weight trend robust and give the daily granularity a real meaning, so `Body` is a trend rather than a scatter of occasional points; (2) daily training means the load series (hrTSS/CTL/ATL) is dense enough for ramp rate and monotony/strain to be meaningful; (3) the weekly review will almost never hit the "insufficient data" path, so that branch stays rare rather than routine; (4) data volume stays small (~365 weigh-ins and a few hundred activities a year — megabytes); (5) consistency is already excellent, which confirms the MVP's value is in the **relationship** rather than in adherence nudges. It also raises the value of the one thing the app adds over the vendors: with daily data on both sides, the load↔body-response correlation is actually computable, which it is not for someone who weighs weekly. | answered |
| **SC-14** | **Engineering time (was Q10)** | **Daily until it is finished.** The owner can work on this essentially every day, not in weekly windows. | The plan can keep real phases instead of being fragmented for a low-cadence owner: hygiene may stay a genuine phase-0 block, phases can be wider, and the calendar between "start" and "used daily" is short. **One invariant is kept anyway, as insurance rather than as pessimism**: every phase must end **usable**, so that if the rhythm changes the thing already built is worth having. An intention is not a guarantee, and the cheapest protection against that is a phase boundary that never leaves the system half-built. | answered |
| **SC-15** | **Strength is a first-class load (from the training workbook)** | The owner's workbook shows strength at roughly **40% of sessions** (`Hipertrofia` 47, `Gym Fuerza` 20, per-quarter gym counts 31/16/14/13/32). Garmin's load is HR-derived and heart rate during strength work is intermittent, so **the load series is systematically understated without it**. Crucially, **no RPE and no `S³` are needed**: the protocol sheets already encode intensity as `%RM` + `RIR` + `sets × reps`, so strength load is computable **deterministically** (tonnage weighted by relative intensity). | `MET-001` gains a strength-load path fed by the protocol definitions rather than by a subjective input — which keeps the system's zero-input property intact. The borrowed `S³` (TrainingGeeks) is **not** adopted: it solves the problem for people who only know duration and perceived effort, and this owner has better data. It also means the load model cannot be "Garmin's number": computed load is canonical (ADR-013 §8), with Garmin's shown as a cross-check whose divergence is *expected* on strength days rather than a bug. | recorded |
| **SC-16** | **The workbook is a source: 19 months of log, a weekly plan and a protocol library** | `Log` holds 517 dated days (2025-01-04 → 2026-08-03) and `Seguimiento` 109 more (2026-08-04 → 2026-11-20): **626 continuous days**, with a quarterly summary block already computed by hand (days logged, training days, ratio, sessions per modality, dominant activity). `Plan Semanal` is a 7-day template with session, type, intensity bucket, **a step target** and a **dinner**; 19 sheets are reusable protocol definitions. | Plan-vs-actual stops being a *later* feature and becomes **the owner's existing workflow made computable** — which raises the question of whether the workbook is ingested as a source or replaced by the app (asked as the next question, and the answer reshapes Block F). The quarterly summary is a reporting requirement that already exists and is cheap to generate: it becomes an MVP report rather than something to invent. **Steps** enter the model (8 000/day target, ≤15 000 weekly average, a rule the owner states competes with muscle recovery), and the `Cena` column records where nutrition would attach when it reopens. | recorded |
| **SC-17** | **Taxonomy drift and planned-vs-completed** | Two years of hand-logging produced **35 distinct activity labels** (`Ergo I/II/III`, `Ergo extensivo`/`2`, `Hipertrofia`/`Fuerza`, `Crossfit I/II/III`, and a `Snow` ×12), and **58 of the 109 current rows are pre-filled future days** — so *logged* is not *happened*. | The schema needs a canonical `session_type` plus the raw `source_label` preserved, and a mapping table — the same provenance discipline as `source` on measurements. It also adds a distinction the design lacked: a session (or a day) is **planned** or **completed**, and the import may only treat past dates as facts. Without this, a backfill would import two months of intentions as history, which is exactly the class of silent error SC-02 was about. | recorded |
| **SC-18** | **The workbook is out of v2 as a data source** | The spreadsheet is the owner's own planning, independent of the app. It is **not ingested, not parsed and not adapted in v2**. In a future version the owner will integrate everything and **replace the Excel with the app** — same intent, same content — but that is a later phase, not this one. | Four consequences. **(1) No xlsx adapter and no xlsx parser in the MVP**, which removes the ~200 lines of fragile parsing (headerless columns, free text, 35 drifting labels, 58 pre-filled future rows) and the brittleness that came with it. **(2) The workbook becomes the requirements document for a future phase** — the app must eventually model what the sheet models (weekly plan with type/intensity/steps/dinner, the protocol library with %RM/RIR/sets, a daily log with deviation notes, and the quarterly summary) — so the data model is designed to be *able* to hold it without building any of it now. **(3) The strength-load path changes, and SC-15 must be read with this caveat**: without the sheet, the `%RM`-based deterministic tonnage is not available, so strength load in the MVP comes from Garmin's `activityTrainingLoad` and is a **known under-estimate that the UI must label honestly** rather than a computed truth. No RPE input is added, so the **zero-input property survives**: Garmin and the scale remain the only two feeds. **(4) A canonical `session_type` is still justified now**, but for Garmin's own `typeKey` zoo rather than for the sheet: charts and per-sport PMC need stable grouping, the raw label is preserved alongside it, and today that mapping wrongly lives in the presentation layer (`frontend/src/lib/format.ts` maps emoji by substring). The mapping table is the same mechanism that later serves the owner's taxonomy. | recorded |
| **SC-19** | The `Sync` page | **Yes — build it** (borrowed from `drkostas/soma`'s Sync Hub, MIT). A page over the `ingest_run` ledger: run history per source, cursor position, rows upserted, last error, last successful run, and the age of the newest data per source. | It answers "why are there no weigh-ins since Tuesday?" in five seconds instead of ten minutes of Loki queries, and it doubles as the operator view while M4 and the first backfill are being verified. It also replaces `sync_log`, which today records a status string per entity type and cannot answer "did last night's run complete". Cost: roughly 170 lines, on top of a ledger that SUB-002 builds anyway. Grafana stays the ops view (research brief 03 finding 13); this page is the product-side answer. | recorded |

## Consequences recorded so far

- **Tier re-definition.** Tier **M'** = the previous S plus the scale pillar, *without* nutrition:
  substrate + metric layer + Today + digest + private deployment + BLE capture + `Body`. Nutrition is
  no longer part of the M definition of done, which removes the largest effort unknown from the MVP.
- **The MVP's reason to exist is now the cross-reference**: body composition (scale) against training
  load and recovery (Garmin), with personal baselines and visible contributors. That is a smaller,
  sharper product statement than "fitness app", and it is testable.
