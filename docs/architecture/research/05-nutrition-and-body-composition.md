# Nutrition, energy expenditure and body composition for garsync

**Bottom line.** A consumer BIA scale measures body weight well and body composition badly: three commercial smart scales missed DEXA fat mass by a median of 2.2–4.4 kg, and a laboratory-grade multi-frequency device still carries a systematic −4.0 % body-fat offset and a 2.6 % standard error of estimate against DEXA ([MEASURED](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8122302/), [MEASURED](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11649400/)). The scale should therefore be modelled as a *weight sensor with a noisy, systematically offset body-composition side channel*: weight and its trend drive the closed loop, and fat/lean/visceral numbers ship as trended ranges with explicit caveats, never as point values. The loop itself should be an adaptive energy-expenditure estimator — intake plus weight trend, back-solved — because that construction is self-correcting against both formula error and logging bias, and the published evidence from the one product that does this at scale (MacroFactor) shows a median expenditure error of ~135 kcal/day after 3–4 weeks versus ~335 kcal/day for TDEE formulas ([MEASURED](https://macrofactor.com/algorithm-accuracy/)). For inputs, do not build a food logger and do not reverse-engineer the FitDays cloud: run `ble-scale-sync` next to the scale, have it export to Garmin Connect, and let garsync's existing `garminconnect` sync pull weigh-ins and body composition from the account it already reads ([MEASURED](https://github.com/KristianP26/ble-scale-sync), [MEASURED](https://github.com/cyberjunky/python-garminconnect/blob/master/garminconnect/__init__.py)).

Evidence labels: **MEASURED** = I fetched the source and read the claim in it. **CLAIMED** = a README/abstract/vendor page asserts it, but I saw it only as a search excerpt or could not open the page. **INFERRED** = my own reasoning or arithmetic, with assumptions stated.

## 1. What a consumer BIA scale actually measures

1. **Weight is the only trustworthy output of a smart scale.** In 53/52/48 hospital patients weighed immediately after DEXA, three consumer smart scales had median absolute weight errors of 0.3 kg, 0.0 kg and 0.25 kg, while fat-mass errors were −2.2 kg (IQR −5.8, 1.3), −4.4 kg (IQR −6.6, 0) and −3.7 kg (IQR −8.0, 0.3), and muscular-mass errors ranged from −6.6 kg to +4.5 kg. The authors' conclusion is unambiguous — "Smart scales are not accurate for body composition and should not replace DEXA in patient care" — and body fat was systematically *underestimated* by all three scales. [MEASURED](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8122302/)

2. **Multi-frequency plus eight electrodes buys precision, not accuracy.** A lab-grade multi-frequency device (InBody 770) in 14 controlled participants had near-perfect test–retest reliability (ICC ≥ 0.998 for %BF, ≥ 0.995 for mass compartments) and small between-day variation (0.1–0.7 kg for mass), yet against DXA it showed a −4.0 ± 2.8 % body-fat bias (SEE 2.6 %) and overpredicted fat-free mass by 2.8 kg. The authors' own fix is an additive +3 % correction, which only moves the bias to −1.0 ± 2.8 %. [MEASURED](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11649400/)

3. **Part of the error is device-family-specific and not yours to fix.** Foot-to-foot consumer scales infer whole-body fat-free mass from a proprietary model trained on DEXA, and error grows with body weight and BMI. [MEASURED](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8122302/) A self-hosted implementation must therefore pick and *declare* a published equation. `ble-scale-sync` documents its coefficients, gates impedance to a 150–1200 Ω plausibility window (outside it, impedance is discarded and body fat falls back to the Deurenberg BMI equation), and states that vendor apps run proprietary dual-frequency models it cannot reproduce — with observed offsets of ~2 points of body fat on at least one device. [MEASURED](https://blescalesync.dev/body-composition)

4. **Hydration and standing time move fat mass by more than a month of real progress.** In 20 adults given 11 mL/kg water after an overnight fast and then measured serially while standing, the added body mass was detected *entirely as fat mass*: final fat mass ~1.3 kg above baseline (~0.9 kg of it immediately after drinking), with body fat +1.5 % in the water condition versus +0.75 % in the no-water control after 65 minutes of standing. The paper recommends roughly 15 minutes of upright rest before assessment to reduce drift. [MEASURED](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9124033/)

5. **Design consequence: gate the reading, then smooth it, then label it.** [INFERRED] A body-fat number that can swing ±1.5 points from a glass of water, on a device whose absolute fat-mass error against DEXA is ~3 points, cannot be rendered as a daily point value. The correct treatment is (a) a collection protocol the app actively encourages (morning, post-void, before food and exercise, barefoot), (b) storage of raw weight *and* raw impedance so any formula can be recomputed retroactively and off-by-one unit errors are auditable, and (c) presentation as a 7-day median inside a 30-day band.

## 2. Energy expenditure: from formula to adaptive estimator

6. **Predictive RMR equations are population tools whose individual errors are large enough to matter.** Which equation wins depends on the population — Mifflin-St Jeor is competitive for general adults, while body-composition-based equations such as Cunningham do better in trained and recreational-athlete samples — and the systematic reviews report that a large fraction of individuals fall outside ±10 % of measured RMR. [CLAIMED](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10687135/), [CLAIMED](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0108460), [CLAIMED](https://www.jandonline.org/article/S0002-8223(05)00149-5/abstract) The vendor simulation published by MacroFactor puts the tail in plain terms: "individual errors of >500 Calories are quite common, and errors exceeding 1,000 Calories per day aren't unheard of." [CLAIMED](https://macrofactor.com/algorithm-accuracy/)

7. **Wearable energy expenditure is not a better answer.** A living umbrella review of systematic reviews covering 249 validation studies found that only ~11 % of commercially released wearables have been validated for at least one biometric outcome, and for energy expenditure the mean absolute percentage error exceeded 30 % across all brands assessed. [MEASURED (abstract)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02077-2), [CLAIMED (PDF abstract)](https://www.jmir.org/2022/1/e30791/PDF) MacroFactor cites a systematic review reporting that in free-living settings wearables misestimate expenditure by more than 10 % in 82 % of cases. [CLAIMED](https://macrofactor.com/algorithm-accuracy/)

8. **The adaptive construction is a back-solve of the energy-balance identity, not a better formula.** Expenditure is inferred from logged intake and the *trended* rate of weight change over a rolling window: `expenditure ≈ mean intake − (Δweight × energy density) / days`. MacroFactor states it as "a deterministic calculation based on your calorie intake and change in trend weight." [CLAIMED](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/dashboard/expenditure) Their version history shows where the engineering effort actually goes: V3 made day-to-day updates ~35 % smaller while picking up durable real trends 1–5 days sooner, and cut weekly absolute weight-change prediction errors by ~15 % versus V2. [MEASURED](https://macrofactor.com/expenditure-v3/)

9. **Data completeness is a hard gate, and the measured threshold is high.** With V2, MacroFactor found it needed 80–85 % intake completeness to keep performing, and that with 2+ missing days per 7 it "was simply better to pause updates." V3 tolerates gaps better but still pauses when more than three intake days are missing in a seven-day period; its imputed intake for unlogged days has a mean error of ~13 % (>90 % of errors below 30 %). [MEASURED](https://macrofactor.com/expenditure-v3/)

10. **How long until the estimate is trustworthy: 3–4 weeks, measured on n=748.** Across 100-day datasets from new users, the median absolute 30-day weight-change prediction error fell from ~1.9 lb in the first month to ~1.15 lb from day 24 onward, while formula-based prediction worsened (2.6 lb → 3.1 lb). In caloric terms, expenditure error after 3–4 weeks of consistent use typically fell in 60–240 kcal/day (median 135) versus 155–590 kcal/day (median 335) for TDEE formulas; over 100 days the correlation between predicted and observed weight change was r = 0.94 (MacroFactor) versus r = 0.61 (formulas). The estimator begins updating on day 3. [MEASURED](https://macrofactor.com/algorithm-accuracy/)

11. **A statistical floor computed here agrees with "four weeks minimum".** [INFERRED] For a straight-line fit of weight over *n* days with independent daily noise σ, the standard error of the slope is `σ / sqrt(n(n²−1)/12)`. At σ = 1.0 kg (free-living; a controlled lab study measured only 0.1–0.7 kg between days [MEASURED](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11649400/)) and 7,716 kcal/kg:

| window | SE of slope (kg/week) | implied SE of expenditure (kcal/day) |
|---|---|---|
| 14 days | 0.46 | ~510 |
| 28 days | 0.16 | ~180 |
| 56 days | 0.06 | ~64 |

Real weight is autocorrelated and non-stationary, so treat these as optimistic. The practical reading — two weeks is barely better than noise, four weeks is useful, eight weeks is good — matches the shape of the MacroFactor data. The energy density used is the 3,500 kcal/lb the vendor applies. [MEASURED for 3500 kcal/lb](https://macrofactor.com/algorithm-accuracy/)

12. **The same mechanism absorbs *logging* bias, which is the real reason to prefer it.** A systematic review of doubly-labelled-water validation studies reports study-level under-reporting of energy intake ranging from 9 % to 47 % depending on population and instrument. [MEASURED (table entries)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2019.00850/full) MacroFactor's authors make the design consequence explicit: when intake carries any measurement error, a more "accurate" recommendation produces *worse* outcomes than one that has absorbed the bias, so the algorithm optimises predictive validity rather than accuracy. [MEASURED](https://macrofactor.com/algorithm-accuracy/) For a single-user self-hosted app this is the load-bearing argument: do not chase label accuracy; let the weight trend correct the labels.

13. **Canonical dynamic models exist if physiology beats back-solving.** Hall's computational model of macronutrient metabolism underlies the NIDDK Body Weight Planner, built precisely because the flat 3,500 kcal/lb rule overestimates weight loss as metabolism adapts. [CLAIMED](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/body-weight-planner), [CLAIMED](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2838532/) MacroFactor uses 3,500 kcal/lb but documents correcting a systematic overestimate that came from assuming asymmetric gain/loss composition. [MEASURED](https://macrofactor.com/expenditure-v3/) For v1, back-solving wins: no population parameters, and it self-corrects.

## 3. Nutrition data acquisition for a self-hosted app

14. **Open Food Facts has the coverage and the licence — and the licence is a real obligation.** The database is ODbL, individual contents are DbCL, images are CC-BY-SA; ODbL requires attribution *and* share-alike, so a database combining OFF with other sources must itself be released openly. [MEASURED](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/data) For a portfolio project this is a first-class design input, not a footnote.

15. **The public API is rate-limited hard enough that a local copy is the design, not an optimisation.** OFF enforces 15 req/min/IP for product reads and 10 req/min/IP for searches, asks consumers needing more than a few hundred products to download bulk exports instead, and reserves the right to IP-ban. [MEASURED](https://openfoodfacts.github.io/openfoodfacts-server/api/) Bulk options are regenerated nightly: a MongoDB dump, an equivalent JSONL (gzip), a compressed CSV (~0.9 GB gz, ~9 GB raw, tab-separated), Parquet on Hugging Face, plus 14-day delta exports for incremental updates. [MEASURED](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/data)

16. **USDA FoodData Central covers the half OFF is weak at — raw and prepared foods — behind a key and a 1,000 req/hour cap.** Every request needs an api.data.gov key; the default limit is 1,000 requests/hour/IP and exceeding it blocks the key for an hour; keys discovered in public repositories are deactivated. The legacy USDA composition API was retired in 2020. [MEASURED](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-guide)

17. **Commercial nutrition APIs are cheap relative to your labour and still the wrong default here.** Edamam lists a US$14/month Enterprise Basic tier with 100,000 calls/month; Nutritionix markets the largest branded database with barcodes and dietitian verification but quotes per-inquiry pricing. [CLAIMED (developer pricing pages seen as excerpts)](https://developer.edamam.com/food-database-api), [CLAIMED](https://www.nutritionix.com/api) For one user, the marginal value over OFF + FDC is small, and the cost is a vendor key plus terms on derived data.

18. **Barcode lookup is OFF's own solved shape; the scanner is front-end work you would own.** OFF documents that its JSON product API is what the official mobile app uses, and the product endpoint is the rate-limited read path. [MEASURED](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/data), [MEASURED](https://openfoodfacts.github.io/openfoodfacts-server/api/) A self-hosted PWA still needs a camera-based EAN/UPC decoder in the browser — I did not verify a specific library, so treat that as unscoped work (see open questions).

19. **Building a food logger is a multi-week project; integrating one is a day.** The prior art shows the cost precisely: NutriTrace is a single-Docker-container tracker (256 stars, AGPL-3.0) with barcode scanning on its Android/PWA client and a documented adaptive-TDEE mode, while wger (6,960 stars, AGPL-3.0) already ships a nutrition diary fed from Open Food Facts. [MEASURED (GitHub API metadata)](https://github.com/TraceApps/nutritrace), [MEASURED (GitHub API metadata)](https://github.com/wger-project/wger)

## 4. The metrics that matter, and how to derive them

20. **Weight trend: an exponentially weighted moving average with α ≈ 0.1 is the boring, documented default.** The Hacker's Diet defines the trend as `Tₙ = Tₙ₋₁ + 0.1(Wₙ − Tₙ₋₁)` — its "10 % smoothing" — and documents the smoothing-constant/lag trade-off it was selected under. [MEASURED](https://fourmilab.ch/hackdiet/www/subsection1_4_1_0_1.html), [MEASURED](https://fourmilab.ch/hackdiet/www/subsection1_2_4_0_7.html) α = 0.1 implies a half-life of ln(2)/0.1 ≈ 6.9 days. [INFERRED] TrendWeight's math page could not be extracted (JS-rendered), and an open issue there argues plain EMA lags during sustained loss and proposes Holt's linear trend instead — a reasonable v2 upgrade, not a v1 requirement. [CLAIMED](https://github.com/ervwalter/trendweight/issues/396)

21. **For the estimator, prefer a regression slope over a fixed window to an EMA, and carry its uncertainty forward.** [INFERRED] An EMA has one tunable number and hides its own error; a weighted least-squares (or Huber-robust) fit over 28–42 days yields both a rate of change and a standard error, which is exactly what the energy-balance back-solve consumes. Recommendation: EMA α = 0.1 for the displayed trend line; robust 28-day slope, cross-checked against 42 days, for the estimator.

22. **Rate-of-change targets are conventional but anchored in a controlled trial.** The elite-athlete comparison used 0.7 % versus 1.4 % of body mass per week, with the slower arm designed to protect lean mass and performance. [CLAIMED (abstract)](https://journals.humankinetics.com/view/journals/ijsnem/21/2/article-p97.xml) Practical v1 bands, stated as defaults rather than laws: cut 0.5–1.0 %/week, maintain ±0.5 %/week, bulk +0.25–0.5 %/week — with the review literature noting larger surpluses add body mass without adding muscle size or strength in the measured outcomes. [CLAIMED](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2019.00131/full), [CLAIMED](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10620361/)

23. **Protein: one number for the surplus, a much higher one for the deficit.** The widely cited meta-regression breakpoint for training-induced fat-free-mass gain is ~1.6 g/kg/day; the primary abstract I retrieved confirms a two-phase breakpoint analysis was performed but does not state the value, so the number is second-hand. [CLAIMED](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/52/6/376) Under energy restriction the systematic review of lean resistance-trained athletes concludes protein needs are "likely 2.3–3.1 g/kg of FFM, scaled upwards with severity of caloric restriction and leanness"; the one non-lean group in that review undergoing substantial restriction consumed 2.5–2.6 g/kg. [MEASURED](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE%3A%22A%20systematic%20review%20of%20dietary%20protein%20during%20caloric%20restriction%20in%20resistance%20trained%20lean%20athletes%22&resultType=core&format=json)

24. **Lean-mass retention justifies the body-composition display — and inherits the scale's error.** [INFERRED] Using 2.3–3.1 g/kg FFM requires knowing FFM, which is exactly the quantity biased by 2.8 kg with a 2.2 kg SEE against DXA on a *lab-grade* device [MEASURED](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11649400/). Resolution: derive the protein target from a slow FFM estimate (30-day median, or a periodic DXA), show the target as a range, and never let a single scale day move a macro target.

25. **Energy availability is a screening flag, not a diagnosis, and must be labelled as such.** The IOC defines `EA = (energy intake − exercise energy expenditure) / fat-free mass` in kcal/kg FFM/day, and its 2023 consensus revisits the historically used 30 kcal/kg FFM/day low-energy-availability threshold rather than asserting it as a universal cut-off. [CLAIMED (consensus excerpt; direct PDF fetch returned HTTP 403)](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/57/17/1073) [INFERRED] At FFM = 60 kg, 30 kcal/kg FFM/day is 1,800 kcal available; a 700 kcal training day on 2,200 kcal intake gives EA = 25 and should raise a *soft* flag. RED-S is a medical diagnosis made by a clinician; the app's wording should be "below the screening threshold", never "you have RED-S", and the flag's width must include FFM's own error.

26. **Control the weekly rate of change, not the daily weight.** [INFERRED] Both the wearable literature (EE MAPE > 30 % [MEASURED](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02077-2)) and the scale literature (fat mass off by 3–4 kg [MEASURED](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8122302/)) argue for one control signal: the weight trend's weekly slope against its target band, with everything else computed from it.

## 5. Closing the loop

27. **Model-driven feedback on weight trend plus intake has been tested prospectively, not just sold.** SmartLoss randomised 40 adults (BMI 25–35) to an intervention that wirelessly transmitted body weight and step data to a server, "used mathematical models to quantify dietary adherence based on body weight," and had counsellors remotely deliver model-driven adjustments, against an attention-matched health-education control. [CLAIMED (abstract text; the full text was not fetchable, so efficacy effect sizes are unverified)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25919921/), [CLAIMED](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2016/1/e18) The underlying construction — a validated dynamic energy-balance model used to *infer adherence from the weight trace* and then adjust the prescription — is exactly the loop garsync would run. [CLAIMED](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4834738/)

28. **The broader intervention evidence is about self-monitoring and feedback frequency.** A systematic review and meta-analysis of self-weighing found it effective for weight loss, with subgroups by weighing frequency and by whether weighing was the isolated intervention or part of a wider programme; a network meta-analysis of web-based interventions classifies them by feedback frequency, personalisation, and human-versus-machine delivery. [CLAIMED](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-015-0267-4), [CLAIMED](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8234624/) Neither full text was fetchable, so I can state direction — frequent, personalised, feedback-rich loops are the effective ones — and nothing about magnitude in a single-user setting.

29. **Adjustment policy should be boring, capped and weekly.** [INFERRED] Synthesising the measured engineering constraints: update weekly; require ≥5 logged days in the prior 7 (MacroFactor's V2 needed 80–85 % completeness [MEASURED](https://macrofactor.com/expenditure-v3/)); label the estimate provisional until 21 days and stable from 28 days [MEASURED for the 3–4 week threshold](https://macrofactor.com/algorithm-accuracy/); move targets by at most ~±150 kcal/week to avoid the over-correction the vendor explicitly engineers against [MEASURED](https://macrofactor.com/expenditure-v3/); change *training volume* only after 2+ consecutive weeks of deviation from the target rate, never on one week's data.

## 6. Prior art worth reading or reusing

30. **`ble-scale-sync` is the single most useful project for this task: it reads a FitDays-family scale locally and writes to Garmin Connect with no phone app.** GPL-3.0, 176 stars, last push 2026-09-14, 35 protocol adapters, TypeScript/Node ≥ 22, with Docker and a Home Assistant add-on. It covers `MGB (Swan / Icomon / YG)` and `Hutbit 218008 / WL292` (documented as "Fitdays app, Lefu OEM"), plus Renpho, Xiaomi, Beurer, Sanitas, Soehnle and others; its Garmin Connect exporter supports back-dating replayed onboard history. [MEASURED (GitHub API + repo/docs)](https://github.com/KristianP26/ble-scale-sync), [MEASURED](https://blescalesync.dev/guide/supported-scales)

31. **The FitDays/Lefu protocol is already decoded in public.** A merged PR documents the Lefu FFB0 "AC02" fixed 8-byte frame (`AC 02 | D0..D3 | STATUS | CKSUM`), sharing service `0xFFB0` with the Robi S9/openScale MGB family, decoded from Android HCI snoops. [MEASURED (PR merged 2026-07-13)](https://github.com/KristianP26/ble-scale-sync/pull/268) A follow-up thread reports raw impedance decoded and command meanings identified on rebranded units. [CLAIMED (issue thread)](https://github.com/KristianP26/ble-scale-sync/issues/322) An earlier request to support an "e-volve" scale whose companion app is FitDays established that weight *and* impedance are broadcast in BLE advertisement packets; that issue was closed as completed in June 2024. [MEASURED (state via GitHub API; resolution detail not read)](https://github.com/theengs/decoder/issues/503)

32. **The FitDays cloud API is reachable only through unofficial clients — a fallback, not a foundation.** Two MIT-licensed reverse-engineered clients target the ICOMON backend: an async Python client (2 stars, last push 2026-09-14) and a TypeScript SDK (3 stars, last push 2026-09-22). [MEASURED (GitHub API metadata)](https://github.com/AboveColin/fitdays), [MEASURED (GitHub API metadata)](https://github.com/roquerodrigo/fitdays-api) No vendor documentation, no stated ToS permission, 2–3 star user base: breakage and account risk that a local BLE read avoids entirely.

33. **`python-garminconnect` already provides both directions of the body-composition path.** The source exposes `get_body_composition(start_date, end_date)` against `/weight-service/weight/dateRange`, plus `get_weigh_ins` and `get_daily_weigh_ins`; it can also write — `add_body_composition(...)` builds a FIT weight-scale record (weight, %fat, %hydration, visceral fat mass, bone mass, muscle mass, BMR, metabolic age, BMI) and uploads it to `/upload-service/upload`, and `add_weigh_in(...)` posts to `/weight-service/user-weight` with `sourceType: MANUAL`. MIT, 3,047 stars, last push 2026-09-18. [MEASURED](https://github.com/cyberjunky/python-garminconnect/blob/master/garminconnect/__init__.py)

34. **The self-hosted adaptive-TDEE prior art is real, and its licence decides what you may do with it.** NutriTrace — AGPL-3.0, 256 stars, last push 2026-09-22 — is a single Docker container with OIDC support, Garmin/Withings/Fitbit/Health Connect integrations, and an adaptive-TDEE mode documented as pulling 35 days of intake and weight and fitting a linear trend. [MEASURED (GitHub API metadata)](https://github.com/TraceApps/nutritrace), [CLAIMED (its own docs site)](https://traceapps.github.io/docs/nutritrace/goals/) OpenScale — GPL-3.0, 2,554 stars, last push 2026-09-22 — is the reference implementation for reverse-engineered scale body-composition algorithms. [MEASURED (GitHub API metadata)](https://github.com/oliexdev/openScale)

35. **Two projects to study but never to copy into a portfolio piece.** SparkyFitness (6,099 stars, last push 2026-09-23) carries a custom licence read from its LICENSE file: use and modification are permitted "for **non-commercial purposes only**", with contributions assigned to the author and automatic termination on commercial use. [MEASURED](https://github.com/CodeWithCJ/SparkyFitness/blob/main/LICENSE) wger and NutriTrace are AGPL-3.0, which is fine to run as a separate service but imposes network copyleft on any code you lift. Waistline (742 stars, last push 2026-09-19) reports **no licence** through the GitHub API, i.e. all rights reserved by default — do not reuse its code. [MEASURED (GitHub API metadata)](https://github.com/davidhealey/waistline)

36. **Smaller adaptive-TDEE implementations exist; all are too young or too dead to depend on.** KalorieKalman (2 stars, MATLAB, last push 2024-02-14, no licence) demonstrates a Kalman-filter formulation of the TDEE problem but is stale and unlicensed. [MEASURED (GitHub API metadata)](https://github.com/jnz/KalorieKalman) `llmn` (1 star, Python, last push 2026-01-01, no licence) combines Hacker's-Diet-style EMA smoothing with a Kalman-filter TDEE learner. [MEASURED (GitHub API metadata)](https://github.com/ddarmon/llmn) Read both for the state-space framing; depend on neither.

## Recommended minimal metric set for v1

**Store daily (all already flowing or one adapter away):** body weight (raw), impedance (raw, when the scale emits it), calories logged, protein grams logged, activity energy expenditure and training load from Garmin, sleep, resting HR, HRV, steps.

**Derive in v1:**

| Metric | Derivation | Presentation |
|---|---|---|
| Weight trend | EMA α = 0.1 over raw weigh-ins | Line, plus raw points as faint scatter |
| Weekly rate of change | Robust slope over 28 days, cross-checked at 42 days | %/week against the goal band |
| Adaptive expenditure | `mean intake − slope × 7716 kcal/kg`, weekly update, gated on ≥5 logged days/7 | Point estimate **with a band** |
| Intake vs target | Logged kcal vs current target | Bar with the target as a line |
| Protein | Logged g/day vs `2.3–3.1 g/kg FFM` in a deficit, ~1.6 g/kg BM otherwise | Range, not a single number |
| Energy availability | `(intake − exercise EE) / FFM` | Soft flag only, labelled screening |
| Lean-mass proxy | FFM from the scale, 7-day median | Trend with a 30-day band, always |

**Show as trends with confidence bands, never as point values:** body-fat %, fat mass, lean/skeletal-muscle mass, visceral fat, bone mass, "metabolic age", scale-derived BMR, the formula-based TDEE seed, and the adaptive expenditure for the first 21 days. **Point values are acceptable for:** weight (with trend overlay), logged calories and protein, steps, sleep duration, and device-derived training load — all of which are direct measurements, not model outputs.

## What this means for garsync

1. **Break the FitDays dependency with hardware, not with a scraper.** Put `ble-scale-sync` on a Raspberry Pi Zero 2W or an ESP32/ESPHome proxy near the scale, enable its Garmin Connect exporter, and keep the existing Garmin sync as the single ingestion path. This converts a phone-app-only data source into a BLE broadcast that garsync already knows how to read, and it removes the ToS/fragility risk of the unofficial ICOMON clients. Confirm first which OEM protocol the scale speaks (see open questions).

2. **Then read body composition from the same client you already use.** `get_body_composition` and `get_weigh_ins` in `python-garminconnect` return exactly what the scale produced, including impedance-derived fields, so v1 needs no new sync code path — only a new table and a new page. [MEASURED](https://github.com/cyberjunky/python-garminconnect/blob/master/garminconnect/__init__.py)

3. **Persist raw inputs, version the formula.** Store weight, impedance, source and timestamp; compute derived body composition in garsync with declared coefficients (the honest precedent is `ble-scale-sync`'s documented equation plus a Deurenberg fallback and a 150–1200 Ω gate [MEASURED](https://blescalesync.dev/body-composition)). When you change a coefficient, recompute history — that is only possible if raw impedance is retained.

4. **Do not write a food logger. Do not buy a nutrition API yet.** The cheapest v1 is a thin private UI over OFF bulk data (Parquet/DuckDB, refreshed weekly) plus USDA FDC for raw foods, with OFF attribution in the UI to satisfy ODbL. If that stalls, the fallback is to *integrate* NutriTrace rather than rebuild it — accepting AGPL-3.0 obligations if you also copy code, which for a portfolio repo means: integrate as a separate container, or reimplement the ideas, never vendor the source.

5. **Make the closed loop weekly and capped.** One scheduled job per week: require ≥5 logged days in the last 7; if not, publish a "insufficient data" state and change nothing. Compute the 28-day slope and expenditure band; adjust the calorie target by no more than 150 kcal; hold macros fixed unless the protein target itself changed. Log every adjustment with the evidence that produced it, so "why did my target change" is answerable from the database.

6. **Reframe the dashboard honestly.** The Body Battery/HRV/sleep half of the dashboard already measures directly; the body-composition half measures a model output. Visually separate them, put the body-composition panel behind a one-line caveat ("BIA estimates; ±3 kg fat mass vs DEXA"), and default to the 7-day median rather than the latest reading.

7. **Keep the RED-S flag conservative and out of the diagnostic register.** It is the one metric here with a plausible path to harm if it is wrong in the optimistic direction, and the FFM denominator is the least trustworthy number in the system.

## Open questions and decisions needed

1. **Which scale exactly?** "FitDays" is an app, not a brand, and support depends on the underlying OEM protocol (Lefu FFB0/AC02, MGB/Swan/Robi family, or broadcast-only variants). Needed: make/model and a photo of the scale's BLE advertisement or its label, so the adapter choice is evidence-based rather than hopeful.
2. **Where does food logging come from?** Three viable paths — a private OFF/FDC-backed UI, integration with NutriTrace, or manual quick-entry of repeated meals — with very different build costs. This is the largest scope decision in the whole plan.
3. **Can the deployment target reach BLE?** If the scale is read directly by a garsync-hosted process rather than through Garmin, the runtime needs a BLE adapter and network access. The nan.builders Basic Space container is a poor fit for that; a Pi near the scale is not. Decide the topology before writing code.
4. **Is Garmin the bus, or is Garmin a peer?** Using it as the bus is pragmatic and zero-code, but it makes an external account the SSOT for body-composition data and adds a sync latency you do not control.
5. **Goal mode for v1:** cut, maintain, or bulk? The rate band, protein target and adjustment logic all key off it, and shipping all three triples the state machine.
6. **Does Manu want RED-S screening at all?** It requires a trustworthy FFM, which the scale does not provide; the honest option is to omit it in v1 or implement it as an explicitly weak flag with the arithmetic shown.
7. **What is the falsification test?** For each derived number, name the observation that would prove it wrong (e.g. a scale reading that disagrees with a clinic scale by >0.5 kg, or 4 weeks where logged intake and trend weight imply an implausible expenditure). Without this, the loop's correctness is unfalsifiable.

**Where the evidence is thin.** The efficacy numbers for SmartLoss were not retrievable (full text not fetchable), so "algorithmic feedback works" rests on the intervention design and the self-weighing literature, not on a verified effect size in a comparable setting. No validation study of a FitDays-branded scale against DEXA was found. The exact protein breakpoint value (~1.6 g/kg) is second-hand. The IOC 2023 consensus text was reachable only as an excerpt.
